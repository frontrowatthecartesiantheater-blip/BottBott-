Right after ChatGPT launched in late 2023, the internet got flooded with pages that hit every keyword and said nothing. Google just came out with an update aimed squarely at that flood, and it's the reason blogs matter more now, not less.

Why Blogging in the Age of AI Matters More Than Ever

A lot of people think blogs are dead in 2026. I think blogging in the age of AI is more relevant than it's been in years, and the timing isn't a coincidence.

The Post-ChatGPT Flood Google Just Started Punishing

In the couple years right after ChatGPT came out, the internet got flooded with generic content mainly used to boost SEO rankings. Businesses figured out they could blast a website with unique pages on every conceivable topic, hit the right keywords, and never actually convey anything of value. It worked for a while. A lot of people spent 2024 doing exactly that.

Google just came out with a big update that included targeting mass-produced AI content, and it's built around what they call EEAT: expertise, experience, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness. That's the exact thing a blog is built to show. A subject matter expert, a business owner, an industry professional sitting down and conveying what they actually know, for the people it matters most to.

So the flood didn't kill blogging. It just made the fake version of it worthless, which means the real version is worth more than it's been in years.

AI Doesn't Get Penalized, Laziness Does

Where people get confused is here. They hear "Google is cracking down on AI content" and assume that means don't use AI tools at all. That's not what's happening.

Google doesn't penalize AI content explicitly. It penalizes the way that content gets used. If you're using AI to quickly produce an authoritative piece, but it's based on your own knowledge, your own experience, your own data, and you're just using the tool to help you get it onto the page, that's not going to get flagged. If you're using it to blast out endless content with nothing behind it, that's a different story, and that's what's getting devalued right now.

Blog content, especially SEO-focused blog content, has to have what you know as its actual foundation. Your personal experience, whether that's running your business or just your own founder story, needs to be the source material, not an afterthought bolted onto an AI draft. That's the whole logic behind a system that turns what you know into blog content instead of one that turns a keyword list into filler. The tool is doing production work. The expertise is still yours.

The Authenticity Algorithms Are Now Rewarding

This isn't just a Google thing. You see the same shift on Instagram, on TikTok, on Facebook, even on LinkedIn. There's an authenticity that people are craving, and the algorithms across every one of these platforms are starting to favor it.

I think people got inundated with bland, generic content and they got tired of it fast. You know what it is when you see it. It's the LinkedIn post that says nothing in five paragraphs. It's the sales copy that could belong to any company in any industry. It's the blog post that answers a question without actually answering it. People can spot it instantly now, and so can the algorithms that decide what gets shown.

Which is genuinely good news if you're a business owner willing to just say what you actually know. The bar is now higher because the noise finally got loud enough that everyone, algorithms included, started filtering for AI signals.

Why LLMS.txt Won't Save You

I hear this one a lot. People want to believe there's a technical shortcut, some file you drop on your site, robots.txt or LLMS.txt, that signals to AI search tools "hey, cite me." I get why the idea is appealing. It sounds like a trick you can implement in an afternoon instead of a strategy you have to actually build.

It doesn't work. OpenAI and Anthropic have said plainly that their models don't read LLMS.txt. So while there's a whole cottage industry telling people to game AI search with these files, the companies building the models are telling you outright they're not looking at them, at least not right now.

Which means there's no shortcut. It comes down to actual SEO and actually being authoritative, the slow way, the way that was always going to work.

Your Blog as the Source of Truth in a Content Ecosystem

SEO is changing, shifting toward long-tail keywords and actual questions instead of short, competitive terms everyone's fighting over. And a blog is the perfect format for that. It's where you answer the specific questions your clients ask, the ones your niche audience actually types into a search bar or asks an AI assistant.

But I don't think the answer is "just focus on blogging" either. What you need is a content ecosystem. Everything you put out, your social posts, your promotion, your repurposed clips and snippets, all of it links back to one source of truth. For most businesses, that source of truth is the website, and on the website, it's the blog. Blogging in the age of AI isn't about grinding out volume anymore. It's about having one place that actually holds your expertise, with everything else pointing back to it.

Starting a Blog in 2026 Is Not Too Late, It's the Point

I know what you're probably thinking. If everyone's already flooded the internet with AI content, why would starting now do anything. But that's exactly backwards. The flood is what got punished. Starting now, with something built on what you actually know, is what the update rewards.

A blog built on real expertise is part of a broader systems approach I build for clients, not a standalone tactic sitting off on its own. It works alongside the rest of your online presence instead of competing with it for attention.

If you've got the knowledge but not the time to turn it into consistent, authoritative content, that's a solvable problem, and I'm happy to talk through what that would actually look like for your business.
